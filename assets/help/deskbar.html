The Deskbar

The Deskbar is the bar TikuDesktop keeps on screen. Its head is the leaf: one button with the TikuOS mark on it and the word TikuOS. Under it come the running applications, one row each, then the tray, then the theme switch, then the clock. The bar starts at the right edge of the screen, and Deskbar preferences, at the foot of this page, changes its shape. The single-window TikuTracker front-end has no Deskbar. See also Settings and the contents.

The leaf menu

A click on the leaf opens the leaf menu, which is built each time it opens from what is on the machine at that moment. Its rows, in the order they are drawn:

  • Find… opens the Find window, the same one Ctrl+F opens
  • Force Quit… selects an unresponsive application to stop
  • Settings holds the preference windows, listed below
  • one row for each application group that has a member: Applications, Programming, Device Tools, Demos
  • a rule, then one row for every place mounted other than the root, opening where it is mounted; the forms a board publishes follow its row, one each, opening that form's panel
  • a rule, then Recent documents, Recent folders and Recent applications, each shown only while its list has something in it, its box in Deskbar preferences is ticked, and its count there is above zero
  • a rule, then the items of the Deskbar folder, .config/deskbar under the home directory or /usr/share/tiku-tracker/deskbar when there is no user folder; the rule is left out when the folder added nothing
  • a rule, then Shut down…

About this system is available from the top-left Tiku menu, and F1 opens Help. These two commands are not repeated in the leaf menu.

Rest the pointer on a row with an arrow for half a second to open its submenu automatically. Clicking the row opens it immediately. Moving to another row starts a fresh delay; moving into the submenu keeps it open. Hovering only opens menus, never chooses a command.

Shut down… also expands on hover, or by clicking its arrow. Clicking the rest of that row puts up the question "Shut down the desktop?" first. Its submenu contains Power off and Restart system, which act only when deliberately chosen.

Settings

The Settings submenu, in the order it is drawn: Deskbar preferences…, TikuTracker preferences…, TikuDesktop preferences…, Fonts…, Sounds…, Devices Preferences…, Startup Preferences…, then Show replicants. That last row names the state it would switch to, so it reads Hide replicants once chosen, and choosing it leaves the menu open with the other word in place. The tray's rows are drawn either way.

The four groups

A program is sorted into a group by the file it is installed as and the folder it is installed in.

GroupWhat it holds
Applicationsevery other .so in apps
Programmingevery .so in apps/programming
Device ToolsBench.so, TikuBench.so, TikuConsole.so, TikuHex.so, and Device Monitor and VFS Inspector, which are built in
Demoshello.so, clock.so, counter.so, sketch.so, twowin.so, sensors.so, gallery.so, demo.so, sounds.so

A row is named after its file: tiku_ goes from the front, underscores become spaces, .so goes from the end and the first letter is capitalised, so editor.so is offered as Editor. Two are renamed after that: DeveloperCalculator.so is Developer Calculator and Bench.so is TikuBench. Rows stand in file-name order, and a group with no member gets no row. Choosing a row starts the program in a process of its own through the tiku-run runner installed beside the desktop; with no runner there, no installed file is offered at all. Device Monitor and VFS Inspector open in the desktop itself.

The clock

In a tall dock the clock is the last thing on the bar, below the tray and the theme switch. It reads the day and month, then the time, as in 13 Sep 20:41. Resting the pointer on it says the whole date in words. A click opens a calendar beside the bar. Four steppers along its head move it a month or a year at a time, a click in the grid picks a day, and a click anywhere else closes it.

A secondary click opens the clock menu, whose four rows each name the state they would switch to: Show clock or Hide clock, Show seconds or Hide seconds, Show day of week or Hide day of week, Show time zone or Hide time zone. The zone is the name the system gives it, or the word local when it has none.

Force Quit

Choose Force Quit… from the TikuOS menu, or press Ctrl+Alt+K while TikuDesktop has keyboard focus. Select a connected application in the list, or press Choose window… and click one of its windows. Escape or a secondary click cancels window selection.

Force Quit… asks for confirmation before terminating the selected application and all its windows. Unsaved work is lost. Cancel is the confirmation's default action; merely selecting an app does not stop it. Refresh takes a new snapshot of connected applications. The displayed PID identifies the selected local process, not a similarly named app.

A desktop-owned window, such as a folder or preferences panel, offers Close Window instead. Device-side windows and unsupported peers cannot be force quit here. Local termination requires Linux kernel support for authenticated process handles; unavailable support disables the action. This tool cannot recover a frozen desktop itself or terminate arbitrary host programs that have no TikuDesktop application connection.

Application rows and their windows

Every application has a row of its own, wearing its own icon, and every window it opens is listed under it, less the application's name; the TikuTracker row holds the desktop's own windows, its folders and panels. Two copies of one application share a row, and the row goes when the last of their windows closes.

The arrow at a row's right end opens the list of that application's windows beneath it, a minimized window marked differently from an open one. On an application row a double click brings that application's windows up, as Show all does, and focuses the top one; Ctrl+click shows all, Ctrl+Shift+click hides all, a middle click starts another copy of the program, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+click opens the Force Quit dialog. On a window row a click raises the window and restores it if it was minimized, Ctrl+click minimizes it, and a secondary click closes it.

A secondary click on an application row opens its window menu: Hide all, Show all, a rule, and Close all. Hide all cannot be chosen while every one of that application's windows is already minimized, nor Show all while none of them is. Hide all minimizes that application's windows, Show all restores them, and Close all closes them; closing windows is never a request to quit, though an application that ends with its last window, as the desk's own do, ends. An application with no window open gets two rows instead: No windows, which cannot be chosen, and Force Quit…, which opens the application selection dialog. The file manager gets only the first of those. In the collapsed docks the whole list folds into a second button under the leaf, which pops a menu of the running applications.

Replicants

In a tall dock the tray sits between the application list and the clock. It holds at most eight replicants. Each is one row: its name on the left, and on the right the present value of a node of the namespace, formatted as that node says it should be read rather than as a raw number.

Dropping exactly one node on the tray subscribes to it, and the status line says so, or says the tray refused it because the name is taken or the tray is full. A replicant whose node has moved is re-pointed to where it went; one whose node is gone is dropped. The list is written to the session store, so it comes back with the next desktop. Dropping on the leaf instead links what was dropped into the Deskbar folder. Started against a device, the desktop puts three readings in the tray itself: uptime, boots and free.

The theme switch

In a tall dock the theme switch has a strip of its own between the tray and the clock, and a press anywhere in that row steps the theme to the next of light, dark and no hue. Along the top or the bottom the switch sits at the far end of the bar, past the last application button.

Deskbar preferences

Opened from Settings. Every change applies to the bar at once and is written down. The rows, in the order they are drawn:

  • Sort applications by name, else rows stand in the order the applications started
  • TikuTracker always first, ahead of whatever the sort says
  • Show application expander, the arrow that opens a row's windows; it can be set only in the tall docks
  • Expand new applications, so an application arrives with its windows already listed; like the row above it, this can be set only in the tall docks
  • Hide application names, leaving the icons; resting on a row then says the name
  • Icon size, stepped by 8 px between 16 and 96, and Font size, stepped by 2 px between 10 and 16
  • Recent documents, Recent folders and Recent applications: a box that puts each list in the leaf menu, and a count of up to 50
  • Always on top, Auto-raise, which can be set only while Always on top is off, and Auto-hide

Below the rows is the dock monitor, labelled Dock location. A click in it moves the bar: the left and right thirds put the bar at that edge, tall when the click is in the middle band and collapsed above or below it to the leaf, the applications button, the tray, the theme switch and the clock; the middle column puts the bar along the top or the bottom. The dock and the bar's width are remembered for the next session. The foot of the window has Defaults, Revert, which restores the settings the window opened with, and Edit in TikuTracker…, which opens the Deskbar folder in a file window.
