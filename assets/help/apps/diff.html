TikuDiff

Shows what changed between two files, two folders, or the two releases two archives hold. Choose Applications > TikuDiff; the window stays blank until you give it something to compare.

Two files

Compare Files... (the two pages, or Ctrl+O) asks for the left file and then the right one. They are shown side by side, a line facing the line it matches: lines taken away are tinted red on the left, lines put in are tinted green on the right, and where a line was changed rather than added or removed, both are tinted and the words that differ inside it are darker. Where one side has lines the other has not, the other side shows an empty band, so the lines below still face each other.

The narrow strip beside the scrollbar maps the whole file: a red mark for each place lines were taken away, green for lines put in, amber for lines changed. Click it to go there. Next Change and Previous Change (the blue arrows, Ctrl+] and Ctrl+[, or N and P) move from one change to the next; the change shown has a blue bar beside it, and the status line says which it is. Up, Down, Page Up and Page Down scroll; Left and Right, or Shift and the wheel, scroll across long lines.

View > Ignore Whitespace compares lines as if a run of spaces were one space and spaces at the end were not there. A line ending in CR LF is always the same as one ending in LF; when the two files end their lines differently, the status line says so.

Two folders, or two archives

Compare Folders... (the two folders, or Shift+Ctrl+O) asks for two folders and lists every file under either: changed, only on the left, only on the right, with their sizes. Files that are the same are left out of the list until View > Show Identical Files is chosen. Double-click a file, or press Return, to see its lines; Back to Folders (the green arrow, Escape, or Ctrl+Up) returns to the list.

Choose two ZIP, tar or tar.gz archives with Compare Files... and TikuDiff opens them into a folder of its own and compares what they hold -- two releases of an SDK, for example. An archive holding only one file, as a .gz does, is compared as that file. The folder is removed when TikuDiff quits.

A folder comparison must finish completely before its results replace the previous comparison. An unreadable file or folder, a damaged archive, or an archive entry that cannot be safely extracted stops the comparison with an error. Directories deeper than 64 levels and paths longer than the supported path buffer are also reported as incomplete.

Keeping the difference

Edit > Copy Differences (Ctrl+C) puts the difference on the clipboard as a unified diff, the form diff -u writes and patch reads; File > Save Differences... (Ctrl+S) writes it to a file. For two folders it is every changed file's difference in one text, each path beginning with a/ and b/ as patch -p1 expects, and a file only on one side added or taken away whole.

Patch export stops if a source file cannot be read, memory runs out, or you cancel. It does not save or copy a partial patch. Saves run in the background and replace the destination only after the complete patch has been written successfully; a failed write leaves the existing file unchanged. Save to a regular file, not a symbolic link.

Open Left... and Open Right... replace one side and keep the other; Swap Sides turns them round.

Limits

Text is shown in the fixed-width face, a tab as four columns. A file over 64 MB, or one holding NUL bytes, is compared byte for byte and only said to be the same or different. Files are compared on this computer: compare a board's files by copying them here first.
