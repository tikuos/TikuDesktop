TikuSDR

TikuSDR shows what a board’s radio hears, laid out as a spectrum analyser: a panel of settings at the left, the spectrogram above, the spectrum on a graticule below it, and a bar at the window’s foot for recording, pausing and what is happening. It is opened from Device Tools in the Deskbar leaf, and shows its announcement for a moment before the window, as TikuBasic does; a key or a click sends it away. About TikuSDR, which the desktop puts in the Tiku menu on the top bar, shows it again. The board does the listening and the arithmetic itself; TikuSDR draws what it sends, can record it to a file and play it back, and exports spectra for a spreadsheet or a script.

It needs an ESP32-C61 whose firmware has the receiver built in (TIKU_DRV_SDR_ESP_ENABLE=1 beside the Wi-Fi driver).

Connect

TikuSDR is opened once an ESP32-C61 is connected through the desktop’s Devices menu, and it borrows the board as soon as it opens: the desktop lets go of it, TikuSDR opens its serial port, as TikuConsole does, and the board lends its receiver a block of memory and brings its radio up; spectra start arriving within a second or two. Closing the window gives the board back. While TikuSDR holds the board, its files and mount are away.

Disconnect in the SDR menu gives the board back without closing the window, to play a recording, and Connect, offered while an ESP32-C61 is connected, borrows it again. The board is chosen in the Device menu, from the ESP32-C61s the desktop has mounted; other boards are not offered.

Opened with none connected, TikuSDR says No software-defined radio detected over its window at once. Its one button, OK, or Return or Escape, closes TikuSDR: connect the radio through the Devices menu, then open TikuSDR again. One connected while the message is up is borrowed at once, the message gone. A recording opened from Tracker needs no board, and no message is shown.

Frequency

Your frequency range, collapsed sections, waterfall height, trace types, averaging, scales, and measurement threshold are saved when TikuSDR closes. Use File > Save Named Setup to keep several setups as .sdrpreset files. The filename is the setup's name. Choose Open Setup to restore one; loading a valid setup closes any recording being played. Setups do not restore receiver gain, arm a trigger, or start recording. The RBW, FFT and rate choices return to automatic when a setup is loaded or a board reconnects.

Click a section heading to expand or collapse its controls. If there is not enough room, other sections collapse to keep the controls visible. Headings also work with Tab and Return. Drag the small handle between the waterfall and spectrum to change their relative heights.

Center, Span, Start and Stop say the view in MHz, and any of them can be typed in: click one, type, and press Return, or Tab or click away. Typing Start or Stop moves Center and Span to match; typing Center or Span moves Start and Stop, the other of the two kept. A Start past the Stop, or a Stop below the Start, moves the other end with the span kept. A number may end in k, M or G, with or without Hz: 2.437G is 2437 MHz. Escape leaves a field as it was. The board tunes 2200 to 2700 MHz, so a view is moved inside that, and is at least 1 MHz wide.

Up to 24 MHz is one spectrum, all of it caught at once, at the narrowest sample rate that holds the view, 4, 8, 10, 20 or 40 MS/s, so a narrow span shows finer bins: 2 MHz about a BLE channel is 15.6 kHz a bin. 24 MHz is what the receiver keeps flat: beyond the middle of a 40 MS/s capture, it hears 15 to 30 dB less. Wider is swept in 16 MHz slices, all heard at the gain the board holds, so they join as one band and a wide signal keeps its height across them; a slice is caught a moment after the one before. A board whose firmware predates the held gain hears each slice at its own automatic gain, and each slice’s noise floor is levelled to a common one instead.

RBW, beside Mode, sets the resolution bandwidth: how close two signals can be and still show as two. It is the 3 dB width of the Hann window the board applies before each FFT, 1.44 times the spacing of its bins. Auto is the finest one capture gives the view -- 225 kHz for 24 MHz, 22.5 kHz for 2 MHz -- and 450 kHz in a sweep. The list offers every resolution the board can give the view with at least ten across it, finer ones by sweeping narrower slices at a lower sample rate: 22.5 kHz across 24 MHz takes nine 4 MS/s slices and arrives twice a second, and Mode then says Sweep. A chosen RBW stays as the view moves, each view getting the rate and FFT size that give it in the fewest bins; a view that would take more than 64 slices goes back to Auto and says so. The list wants the board’s capabilities, read as it connects; RBW (Cycle) in the SDR menu steps through it.

The frequency panel shows Mode: Live for a single capture or Mode: Sweep for sequential captures across a wider band. Click it to switch modes. This is independent of the Run/Pause button and the connection status at the bottom. The Live menu command goes back to the last view of the other kind: from a live view to the last wide one -- the 2.4 GHz band, 2396 to 2492 MHz, until another is typed -- and from a wide view to the last live one. In a sweep, click the spectrogram -- or the spectrum, with no marker out -- to see 24 MHz there, live.

Traces

For tuning gestures, see Zoom and pan below.

Three traces can be shown at once, each in its own ink: trace 1 black, trace 2 red, trace 3 blue. Pick one in Trace, then what it shows in Type:

  • Clear & Write -- the latest spectrum, as it arrives.
  • Average -- the spectra averaged, each new one weighed as one of Avg count (1 to 100): the higher, the steadier and the slower.
  • Max Hold and Min Hold -- the highest and the lowest level heard at each frequency since the type was picked or Clear.
  • Off -- not shown.

Trace 1 opens as the average of four spectra, the others off. Clear & Write on one trace with Average on another draws the average over the latest, and Max Hold beside them shows the peaks either has reached. Clear starts every trace again from the latest spectrum; Export is the File menu’s Export CSV. The Trace menu holds the same choices.

Saved reference traces

Select a trace, then choose File > Save Trace as Reference. This saves the selected trace's current values, including an average or hold, to a .sdrref file. Open Reference Trace loads it; Trace > Show Reference Trace toggles its purple overlay.

A reference is drawn only when its frequency grid, sample rate and FFT size match the current acquisition. A different grid produces a visible message instead of a misleading overlay. Values retain floating-point precision rather than the waterfall's half-decibel storage. The file also keeps the board name and reported gain. Levels are relative, not calibrated dBm: changing boards, gain behavior, antennas or receiver conditions can affect the comparison.

Markers

Ordinary left-button drags move markers. Shift-drag selects a tuning range instead; Alt/Option-click on the waterfall inspects history.

Four markers read a trace’s level at a frequency. Pick one in Marker, the trace it reads in On trace, and check Active: it comes out on the highest peak no other marker stands on. Its frequency is in Freq, where one can be typed; its readings stand in the graticule’s top left corner, each marker after the first with how far it is from M1, in MHz and dB. The selected marker is drawn in green, with a dotted line down the graticule.

Peak Search moves the selected marker to the highest peak, Next Peak to the highest one below it, and Peak Left and Peak Right to the nearest peak that way. A peak is a level that rises 6 dB above the lowest ground between it and anything higher. To Center moves the view so the marker is its centre, its span kept, and All Off takes every marker in.

On the spectrum, press near a marker to take it up, or anywhere else to bring the selected one there, and drag; let go and it climbs onto the peak it is beside. While Track is checked, markers follow the peak they stand on as it drifts from one spectrum to the next. The Marker menu holds the same commands.

Reading it

Channel activity, triggers, capture health and how to read the graticule are described on their own page, Reading it.

Receiver gain

Expand Receiver while connected to read the current gain setting. You can also click Read receiver gain. Controls become available only after the firmware confirms its setting. Enable Automatic gain to use the receiver's AGC, or enter a whole-number Index from 0 to 255 to hold the gain. Disabling automatic gain selects index 60. These are hardware indices, not calibrated gain values in decibels.

Changes wait for the active capture to finish. The field shows the confirmed setting, not an unacknowledged request. A confirmed gain change clears the old traces and waterfall; recordings include a view reset at that point. Capture protection may still reduce gain for a strong signal, so the gain displayed above the spectrum can be lower than the requested index. Unsupported or unconfirmed commands disable gain editing until a successful read. These settings are not saved as desktop preferences.

Recording and playing back

The File menu holds what reaches a file: recordings, CSV and pictures.

Record, the red dot in the bar, while a board is connected, asks where to save and then writes every spectrum the board sends, with its time, until it is pressed again, the board is disconnected or the window closes. The bar shows how long and how large the recording has grown, about 20 to 30 MB an hour. The name offered is the board’s and the minute it began, ending in .sdrlog.

Open Recording…, with the board given back by Disconnect, plays a recording back at the pace it was made, through the same spectrogram and spectrum. While it plays, the view is the recording’s own, so the frequency settings are off; pausing, the traces and the markers work as they do live, and Connect goes back to the board. A silence longer than two seconds, such as the live view paused while recording, plays in half a second. A recording double-clicked in Tracker opens in TikuSDR and plays at once.

Click the timeline to seek. TikuSDR rebuilds the trace history up to that time in small batches, then pauses; click Run to continue. Long recordings can take time to seek, but the window remains responsive. The timeline also works after playback reaches the end.

The File menu provides Back 10 Seconds, Forward 10 Seconds, and Next Frame. Next Frame pauses and advances through one complete spectrum or sweep. Speed cycles through 0.25x, 0.5x, 1x, 2x and 4x playback. Turn off Skip Long Gaps to preserve recorded timing instead of shortening long silences. Seeking and frame stepping use the recorded timestamps regardless of that option. Connection, pause, and recording status stay visible while inspecting the plots with the pointer.

A recording is plain text, so a script can read it too. Each line after its header is the time in seconds since the start, then what the board sent: SPEC with the centre in megahertz, the sample rate, the gain step, the number of bins, and two hex digits a bin from the lowest frequency up, in half-decibels, then held when the board held the gain; SWEEP where a sweep ends; and VIEW where the view changed.

CSV and pictures

Export CSV… writes spectra as rows of three columns, the time in seconds, the frequency in megahertz and the level in decibels, for a spreadsheet or a script. With a recording on screen it writes the whole recording, a line of its progress in the bar as it goes; otherwise it writes the spectra the spectrogram holds, timed from the oldest. A minute of Live is about 150 000 rows.

Save Picture… saves the spectrogram and the spectrum, with their annotations and markers, as a PNG.

Zoom and pan

Over the spectrum or waterfall, turn the wheel to zoom around the pointer. Hold Shift while turning the wheel to move left or right by a tenth of the current span. Hold Shift and drag with the left button to select a frequency range; release to tune to that range. Drag with the middle button to pan. The highlighted preview does not retune the receiver until you release it. Escape cancels a drag without closing TikuSDR.

Zoom In, Zoom Out, and Previous View are in the SDR menu. The keyboard equivalents are +, -, and B. Previous View remembers up to 16 tuning ranges. These operations respect the receiver's frequency and span limits. They change acquisition, not just the drawing: existing traces and waterfall history start afresh. They are disabled while a recording is displayed. Use the recording's timeline and history inspection instead; no additional frequency detail can be recovered from the saved spectra.

Keys

Space pauses and resumes; L presses Live; the arrow keys move the view one megahertz, five with Shift. P makes trace 2 a max hold or takes it off, A does the same for the latest on trace 3, and C clears the traces. 1 to 4 pick a marker, M puts it out or takes it in, K is Peak Search, N Next Peak, [ and ] the next peak left and right, and Delete takes it in. W switches the spectrogram, R starts and stops a recording, and Escape closes the window.
