TikuSDR

TikuSDR shows what a board’s radio hears: a live spectrum and, beneath it, a waterfall of the spectra that came before. It is opened from Device Tools in the Deskbar leaf. The board does the listening and the arithmetic itself; TikuSDR draws what it sends, can record it to a file and play it back, and exports spectra for a spreadsheet or a script.

It needs an ESP32-C61 whose firmware has the receiver built in (TIKU_DRV_SDR_ESP_ENABLE=1 beside the Wi-Fi driver).

Connect

TikuSDR is opened once an ESP32-C61 is connected through the desktop’s Devices menu, and it borrows the board as soon as it opens: the desktop lets go of it, TikuSDR opens its serial port, as TikuConsole does, and the board lends its receiver a block of memory and brings its radio up; spectra start arriving within a second or two. Closing the window gives the board back. While TikuSDR holds the board, its files and mount are away.

Disconnect in the SDR menu gives the board back without closing the window, to play a recording, and Connect, offered while an ESP32-C61 is connected, borrows it again. The board is chosen in the Device menu, from the ESP32-C61s the desktop has mounted; other boards are not offered.

Opened with none connected, TikuSDR says No software-defined radio detected over its window at once. Its one button, OK, or Return or Escape, closes TikuSDR: connect the radio through the Devices menu, then open TikuSDR again. One connected while the message is up is borrowed at once, the message gone. A recording opened from Tracker needs no board, and no message is shown.

Live and Band

Live shows 40 MHz around one frequency in 256 bins, about ten spectra a second. The receiver’s filter is flat over the middle 24 MHz; the shaded edges are its roll-off, not the air. The receiver’s own leakage at the tuned frequency is no signal, so the few bins on it show their neighbours’ level. Tune with -5 MHz and +5 MHz, the arrow keys (one megahertz, five with Shift), the scroll wheel, or the Tune menu’s Wi-Fi and BLE channels.

Band shows the whole 2.4 GHz band, 2396 to 2492 MHz, stitched from six slices, about five sweeps a second. Each slice is heard at its own automatic gain, so its noise floor is levelled to a common one before the slices are joined: in Band, a level is a height above the floor around it. Click anywhere in the band to tune Live there.

Reading it

The bright trace is the average of recent spectra (Average in the SDR menu); the amber trace holds each frequency’s peak while Peak is held down, until Clear in the SDR menu. Beneath the plot are the band’s channel plans: Wi-Fi channels 1 to 13, BLE’s advertising channels 37, 38 and 39 (also dashed in the plot), and 802.15.4 channels 11 to 26. Point at the plot or the waterfall to read a frequency’s level and the channels it falls in.

Levels are the board’s own decibels, not calibrated to the air, and the receiver’s automatic gain moves them; compare shapes and neighbours rather than absolute numbers. Wi-Fi frames are bursts twenty megahertz wide; BLE advertising and 802.15.4 are narrow, two megahertz.

Recording and playing back

The File menu holds what reaches a file: recordings, CSV and pictures.

Record, while a board is connected, asks where to save and then writes every spectrum the board sends, with its time, until Record is pressed again, the board is disconnected or the window closes. The status line shows how long and how large the recording has grown, about 20 to 30 MB an hour. The name offered is the board’s and the minute it began, ending in .sdrlog.

Open Recording…, with the board given back by Disconnect, plays a recording back at the pace it was made, through the same spectrum and waterfall. While it plays, the view is the recording’s own, so tuning is off; Pause, Peak, Average and Clear work as they do live, and Connect goes back to the board. A silence longer than two seconds, such as the live view paused while recording, plays in half a second. A recording double-clicked in Tracker opens in TikuSDR and plays at once.

A recording is plain text, so a script can read it too. Each line after its header is the time in seconds since the start, then what the board sent: SPEC with the centre in megahertz, the sample rate, the gain step, the number of bins, and two hex digits a bin from the lowest frequency up, in half-decibels; SWEEP where a sweep of the band ends; and VIEW where the window changed between Live and Band.

CSV and pictures

Export CSV… writes spectra as rows of three columns, the time in seconds, the frequency in megahertz and the level in decibels, for a spreadsheet or a script. With a recording on screen it writes the whole recording, a line of its progress in the status line as it goes; otherwise it writes the spectra the waterfall holds, timed from the oldest. A minute of Live is about 150 000 rows.

Save Picture… saves the spectrum, the channel plans, the waterfall and the status line, everything below the toolbar, as a PNG.

Keys

Space pauses and resumes, B shows the band, L returns to Live, P toggles the peak hold, C clears the peaks and the average, R starts and stops a recording, and Escape closes the window.
