TikuSDR: reading it

How to read what TikuSDR shows, and the measurement tools that work on it. The rest of the application is described on the TikuSDR page.

Channel activity

Choose Trace > Channel Activity, or expand Measurements and press Channel Activity. Activity bars replace the waterfall; press it again to return. Channels cycles through Wi-Fi channels 1–13 (20 MHz), BLE channels 0–39 (2 MHz), and 802.15.4 channels 11–26 (2 MHz). In a narrow window, Next Page shows the remaining channels. Set Threshold in relative dB in Measurements.

A completed capture counts as active for a channel if any bin in that channel reaches the threshold. Only channels fully inside the captured frequency range count. n/a means not observed, not zero activity. The percentage is active observations divided by total observations: it is not airtime, packet count, or proof of Wi-Fi/BLE/802.15.4 traffic. Channel bands overlap, and a sweep observes its slices at different times. The heading reports the elapsed span and maximum observation count; Export Activity CSV includes each channel's own counts and span.

Changing range, FFT, rate, gain mode or threshold resets the counts. Reset Activity Counts starts a new observation period. During playback, observations use recorded timestamps; seeking rebuilds counts from the beginning to the selected time. Looping rebuilds them each pass. Older automatic-gain sweep recordings are noise-floor-normalized, so their activity percentages should not be compared with held-gain recordings.

Receiver controls and capture health

At connection, TikuSDR asks sdr info for supported sample rates, FFT sizes and tuning limits. Expand Receiver to use Read Capabilities, FFT and Rate. Each button cycles through supported choices that fit the current range, including automatic selection. The decoder supports 64-, 128- and 256-point FFTs and rate codes for 4, 8, 10, 20, 40 and 80 MS/s. Sweeps keep the existing 40 MS/s, 16 MHz-step acquisition method; a rate override cannot change that method. An unsupported range pauses requests and displays an explanation.

If firmware does not report capabilities, acquisition uses the legacy automatic settings and the override buttons stay disabled. Gain controls still require their own confirmed reply. Acquisition changes are stored in version 3 recordings; current TikuSDR can also read versions 1 and 2.

SDR > Show Capture Health displays the receive state, rejected spectra, incomplete sweeps and local receive-buffer overflows in the transport bar when the pointer is not inspecting a plot. STALE means no complete frame for more than three seconds while running; a long sweep can also cause this indication. Rejections include obsolete replies after a range change. These are local diagnostics, not radio packet-loss statistics. Clipping is shown as not reported because current firmware does not supply a clipping counter; a high spectral value alone does not prove clipping.

Triggered recording

Connect and run the receiver, then expand Measurements. Set the threshold, Pre (s) (0–30 seconds), and Post (s) (1–300 seconds). Choose Arm Trigger and a destination. The destination is opened immediately, so write errors are reported before waiting for an event. The trigger is one-shot: the first complete frame with any bin at or above the threshold starts the capture, including retained history.

Pre-trigger history is limited to 256 protocol lines, or the requested duration, whichever is shorter. A sweep uses several lines; retention therefore depends on acquisition speed and range. History starts at a complete-frame boundary. Recording stops after the post-trigger duration, even if no further frames arrive. Changing acquisition, range, gain, threshold or trigger timing disarms the trigger; disconnecting or closing the app also closes its file. Disarm / Finish Capture or the Record stop button ends it manually. If no event occurs, the file contains only recording metadata, not captured spectra. There is no automatic re-arming or overwrite on a later event.

Bookmarks and playback loops

With a recording open, enter a label in Measurements > Bookmark and press Bookmark. Up to 32 labelled positions are supported; red ticks mark them on the timeline. Next Event seeks to the next bookmark and wraps to the earliest. Clear Bookmarks removes the annotations, not the recording.

Seek to the beginning of an interval and choose File > Set Loop Start Here. Seek to its end and choose Set Loop End Here, then Loop Selected Interval. A green line marks the interval. Looping uses original timing rather than skipping gaps and works after playback has reached the end. Seeking is performed in bounded batches; a long recording can take a moment to rebuild before each repeat.

Bookmarks and interval endpoints are saved beside the recording as recording.sdrlog.sdrmarks. Keep that file with the recording when moving it. The recording itself is unchanged. An unwritable folder produces a save error; the annotations remain available for the current session. Looping is not automatically enabled when reopening a recording.

The waterfall's time labels show seconds before the newest capture. Each pixel row represents one capture, not a fixed time interval. Dashed horizontal lines mark intervals longer than two seconds between captures; they do not imply that the radio observed the signal during that interval. Pointing at a waterfall row shows that row's stored level and age, rather than the latest spectrum's level.

Hold Alt (Option on macOS) and turn the wheel over the waterfall to browse older or newer rows. Alt/Option-click a row to overlay its spectrum in green, labelled History overlay; live traces and their markers remain separate. The selected capture stays selected as new captures arrive. The history holds at most 480 captures for the current frequency range; the overlay disappears when its capture is overwritten. This display history has half-decibel precision and levels stored between 0 and 127.5 dB.

The SDR menu also offers Older Waterfall Rows, Newer Waterfall Rows, and Return to Latest. Return to Latest scrolls back to the newest capture and removes the historical overlay. History inspection works with live data, paused acquisition, and recordings. Changing the frequency range or seeking resets the displayed history.

The graticule has ten divisions each way. Ref, above at the left, is the level of its top line, and Div the decibels a division: 2, 5, 10 or 20, chosen to hold the traces shown, and changed only when they leave the room it gives them. Bin is the spacing between FFT points, with the sample rate or the slices beneath; the receiver’s gain and which trace is which stand at the right. Beneath the graticule are Start, Center, Stop and Span, the number of points, and how many spectra or sweeps arrive a second. A recording made before TikuSDR swept past 24 MHz may show 40 MHz in one spectrum; its edges, beyond the middle 24 MHz, are shaded.

Expand Display to set Ref and dB/div yourself. Editing either turns off automatic spectrum scaling. Re-enable Auto spectrum scale to fit the traces again. The waterfall has separate Floor and Ceiling fields: editing either locks its colour scale, so the same level keeps the same colour across captures. Use Auto waterfall scale to restore automatic colours. Levels must be between -200 and 300 dB, with the floor below the ceiling. Bin spacing is not a calibrated resolution bandwidth: the receiver applies a Hann window before the FFT.

The spectrogram shows the spectra that came before, newest at the top, coloured from just under the noise, blue, through green and yellow to red at the reference level; the scale stands at its left. Point at either to read a frequency, each trace’s level there and the channels it falls in.

Spectrogram in Display switches the spectrogram off, the spectrum taking its room, and Channel plans shows the band’s plans beneath the graticule: Wi-Fi channels 1 to 13, BLE’s advertising channels 37, 38 and 39 (also dashed on the graticule), and 802.15.4 channels 11 to 26.

Levels are the board’s own decibels, not calibrated to the air; compare shapes and neighbours rather than absolute numbers. The board holds its receiver’s gain still, at step 60, rather than letting it chase every burst, so a level stays put from one spectrum to the next; a capture loud enough to clip is taken again 16 steps lower and raised back by as many decibels. Gain is the step it was heard at, or says auto on a board whose gain still moves on its own. Wi-Fi frames are bursts twenty megahertz wide; BLE advertising and 802.15.4 are narrow, two megahertz.
