TikuPDF

A reader for the PDFs this desk works with: datasheets, reference manuals and papers. Choose Documents > TikuPDF, or double-click a PDF in Tracker; PDFs (.pdf, in any case) open in TikuPDF unless File Associations names another reader. TikuPDF also appears in Open With.

Opening a document

Choose Open (the folder at the left of the toolbar) or press Ctrl+O. The toolbar's buttons are icons, as elsewhere on the desktop; rest the pointer on one and it says what it does, and its shortcut. The document opens on a background worker, so a datasheet of a thousand pages is ready in a moment and the window never waits on it. If a file cannot be opened the status line says why — not a PDF, or damaged beyond repair — and the document already shown stays.

Encrypted PDFs open like any other when they need no password, as most do. A PDF locked with a password shows a separate password panel below the toolbar. Type the password and press Enter or click Unlock. Click Cancel, or press Escape in the password field, to leave it closed. The Find field remains separate. A password may be in any script, and still matches when an accent is typed as a mark of its own, letters come full width, or a space is an odd one. The password is used once and not kept. A document whose author does not allow its text to be copied can still be read and searched; Copy says so instead of copying.

A PDF sealed to certificates opens only for the people it was sealed to, with their own key. TikuPDF asks for your key file at once — a .p12 or .pfx file, or a PEM file, holding the RSA key of your certificate — and then asks for the key's password in the password panel (just Enter when it has none). File > Open Key... chooses another key file. The status line says when the password does not open the key, or the key is not one the PDF was sealed to. The key and its password are used once and not kept.

TikuPDF remembers where you left each document — the page, how far down it, the zoom and the turn — and opens it there again. File > Open Recent lists the documents read lately; Clear Menu forgets them and their places. They are kept in TikuPDFRecent in your settings folder (~/.config), never in the documents.

Use Bookmarks > Bookmark This Page or Ctrl+D to save a page explicitly. The Bookmarks menu lists saved pages for the current PDF. Selecting one restores its position, zoom and rotation. Use the same command on a bookmarked page to remove it. Up to 24 bookmarks are kept across documents in TikuPDFBookmarks in your settings folder; edits from separate viewer windows are combined. If another window is saving bookmarks at that moment, nothing changes and the status line asks you to try again. If bookmarks cannot be saved, changes are kept in that window until it closes, and the status line says so. These bookmarks do not modify the PDF or its outline.

Reading

  • Pages follow one another; scroll with the mouse wheel, the arrow keys, Page Up and Page Down, or Space and Shift+Space. Home and End go to the first and last page.
  • Fit Width fits the pages to the window's width and follows it as the window is resized; Fit Page fits a whole page. Zoom In and Zoom Out, the magnifiers (or Ctrl+mouse wheel), step the zoom from 25% to 400%; Ctrl+0 shows the actual size.
  • Type a page number in the field between the green arrows and press Enter to go to it; the arrows, Previous Page and Next Page, step a page. Back returns from a link or an outline entry.
  • Rotate Right (Ctrl+R) and Rotate Left (Ctrl+L), on the toolbar and in the View menu, turn every page a quarter at a time, for the drawing printed sideways; the status line shows the turn. Selecting, finding and links work on the turned pages as on upright ones.
  • Pages are drawn as you reach them; a page not drawn yet is white, and after a zoom the old drawing stands in until the new one is ready.
  • If a page could not be rendered, choose View > Retry Failed Pages. Failed pages are retried as they come into view, without closing the document or losing your place.

Outline, pages and links

Outline shows the document's own table of contents beside the pages; click an entry to go there, or its + and - to fold it. The entry for the part you are reading stays marked as you scroll. Pages (Thumbnails in the View menu) shows every page drawn small instead, the one you are on ringed (these two and Signatures are the toolbar's second group); click one to go there, or use the arrow keys, Home and End once the column has been clicked. Links in the page work too: the status line says where one leads as the pointer passes over it, and a click follows it. Ctrl+[ goes back to where you were. A link to a web address is copied to the clipboard instead, since no browser is started from here.

Finding and copying

Ctrl+F goes to the Find field. Enter searches the whole document, a stretch of pages at a time, so you can read on while it looks; the first match after where you are is shown as soon as it is found. Enter or Ctrl+G goes to the next match, Shift+Enter or Shift+Ctrl+G to the previous. Case, and the different kinds of space, hyphen and quote a typeset page uses, do not matter; a phrase broken over two lines is still found. Match Case and Whole Words, in the Edit menu, narrow the search and run it again. Search retains up to 5,000 matches; when that limit is reached, the status asks you to narrow the search rather than claiming the results are complete. A page-reading, memory, or worker failure is reported as an incomplete search. Press Enter in Find to retry the same query. Existing matches remain available until you retry.

Drag across text to select it and press Ctrl+C to copy it. Ctrl+A selects the text of the page you are on. The clipboard holds 16 MB of text, room for the whole of a long document; copying more takes the first 16 MB and the status line says so.

Signatures

A signed document opens with Signatures (the red seal) held down: the column beside the pages shows each signature, in the order they were made, and the signed fields on the pages are tinted by what was found. For each, TikuPDF checks that the part of the file it signs is exactly what was signed, by the key of the certificate the signature carries, and whether anything was added to the file after it.

  • Integrity verified: nothing it signs has changed. Intact; added to: what it signs is intact, but the document was added to after the last signature — a filled-in form, or other changes.
  • Document changed: what it signs is not what was signed. Not valid: the signature is not its certificate's key's. Not checked: it is made in a way TikuPDF does not read.
  • Each shows who signed, their organisation, when (from a timestamp authority when there is one, else the signer's own clock, in UTC), and the reason and place they gave. A click goes to the signature's field.

Signer identity is unverified. The integrity result does not establish that the signer is who the certificate names. A certificate anyone can make says any name; TikuPDF keeps no list of the authorities it would trust, and each card says whose certificate it is. Signatures by RSA (PKCS#1 and PSS) and ECDSA, in the forms PDFs use (adbe.pkcs7.detached, ETSI.CAdES.detached, adbe.pkcs7.sha1, adbe.x509.rsa_sha1) and document timestamps, are checked.

What is not drawn yet

Shadings of every kind, repeating patterns, transparency (blend modes, soft masks, and groups, knockout ones too), faxed, JBIG2, JPEG 2000 and progressive JPEG pictures (the scans of older datasheets), and layers shown or hidden as the document says are drawn as the document has them. A JPEG 2000 picture too large to hold whole is drawn from a smaller resolution of itself. Annotations and filled-in forms show their own appearance, or when they carry none, the one a viewer gives their kind. Forms show what was filled in but cannot be edited here. Fonts a PDF names without carrying are drawn with the desktop's own faces, at the widths the document gives them; Chinese, Japanese and Korean with the machine's faces for each, and text written down the page is drawn and read in columns. Some older Chinese, Japanese and Korean documents name their encoding without carrying it; those are read in full where poppler-data is installed, and otherwise only as far as the document itself says.
